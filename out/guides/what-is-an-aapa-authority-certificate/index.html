What is an AAPA Authority Certificate and do I need one?

By Helen Haritos, Consultant Anthropologist, Connected2Country · Updated 2026-11-04

An Authority Certificate is a document issued by the Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority (AAPA) under the Northern Territory Aboriginal Sacred Sites Act 1989. It sets out the conditions under which proposed works can proceed without damaging or interfering with a sacred site, and it is a complete defence to prosecution under the Act if the works stay within those conditions.

Why it matters

Sacred sites are protected everywhere in the Northern Territory, on all tenures, whether or not they are registered and whether or not you know they are there. Damaging one is a criminal offence with penalties for both the company and individuals. The certificate is the only way to know in advance where the constraints are.

When to apply

  • Any ground-disturbing works where sacred sites are plausible: coastal land, rivers and creeks, springs, rock outcrops, significant trees, and most undeveloped land around Darwin and Palmerston.
  • When a development permit, NT EPA approval, land council agreement or contract requires it.
  • When your financier, principal or insurer asks for it, which is increasingly common.

How the process works

  1. Application to AAPA with a description of the works and a map of the area. Fees are set against project value.
  2. AAPA checks its register and records and consults the custodians of any sites in or near the area, usually with field visits.
  3. AAPA issues the certificate with conditions, often including restricted work areas (RWAs) with buffers and limits on particular activities.
  4. The proponent builds the conditions into its management plans, inductions and site controls.

Common mistakes

  • Applying over too small an area, then needing a new certificate when the design changes.
  • Describing the works too narrowly, so that laydown areas, haul roads or borrow pits are not covered.
  • Treating the certificate as a tick-box and never translating the conditions for the site crew.
  • Leaving the application until after the program is set. Custodian consultation takes the time it takes.

Do I need Traditional Owner consultation for my Darwin development?

By Helen Haritos, Consultant Anthropologist, Connected2Country · Updated 2026-11-05

For most development in the greater Darwin region the answer is yes, and the real question is how much and how early. The Larrakia people are the recognised Traditional Owners of the Darwin region, and several approval pathways either require their involvement or work far better with it.

Approvals that expect consultation

  • AAPA Authority Certificates are issued on the basis of consultation with custodians of sacred sites.
  • NT EPA assessment of significant projects requires the proponent to assess impacts on culture and heritage, which cannot be done credibly without talking to Traditional Owners.
  • Heritage Act 2011 processes for Aboriginal archaeological places and declared heritage places involve the Heritage Council and, in practice, Traditional Owner views.
  • Native Title future act procedures apply where native title has not been extinguished.
  • Planning Act processes for rezoning and large subdivisions increasingly expect evidence of consultation, and Crown land and Darwin Waterfront, Defence and Commonwealth processes have their own requirements.

Who to consult

Identifying the right people is anthropological work, not a mailing list. Larrakia country has family groups with particular connections to particular places, and some areas on the edges of the region are shared with or claimed by neighbouring groups. Getting this wrong is the most common reason consultation is later challenged.

What good consultation looks like

  • Started early, before the design is fixed.
  • Plain information about what is proposed, with maps and site visits.
  • Time for people to talk among themselves and come back.
  • Records that show who was consulted, what they said and what changed as a result.
  • Fair payment for people's time and expertise.

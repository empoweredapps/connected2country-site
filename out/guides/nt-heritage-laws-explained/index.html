NT heritage laws explained: Sacred Sites Act, Heritage Act and the Land Rights Act

By Helen Haritos, Consultant Anthropologist, Connected2Country · Updated 2026-11-05

Three pieces of legislation do most of the work in protecting Aboriginal cultural heritage in the Northern Territory. Projects often need to deal with more than one.

Northern Territory Aboriginal Sacred Sites Act 1989

Protects sacred sites, which are places of significance under Aboriginal tradition, on all land in the Territory. Administered by the Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority (AAPA), which keeps a register, consults custodians and issues Authority Certificates. Offences apply whether or not a site is registered.

Heritage Act 2011 (NT)

Protects declared heritage places and objects, and gives automatic protection to all Aboriginal and Macassan archaeological places and objects. Administered by the Heritage Branch with advice from the NT Heritage Council. Works that may affect a protected place need approval, and unexpected finds must be reported.

Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976 (Cth)

Grants inalienable freehold title to Aboriginal land trusts over roughly half of the Territory and requires that Traditional Owners consent, through the relevant land council, to leases, mining and other uses of that land. Section 19 leases and section 42 to 48 mining provisions are the parts proponents meet most often. Land councils employ anthropologists to identify Traditional Owners and run consultations.

And the rest

The Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) applies where native title survives on non-Aboriginal land; the Environment Protection Act 2019 (NT) requires culture and heritage to be assessed for significant projects; and the Commonwealth Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Heritage Protection Act 1984 is a last-resort protection.

How they fit together on a project

A subdivision on freehold land near the Darwin coast might need an AAPA Authority Certificate, a Heritage Act check for archaeological places, and Traditional Owner consultation for the NT EPA or planning process. A road on Aboriginal land near Maningrida needs land council consent under the Land Rights Act plus the certificate. Working out the pathway early is most of the value an anthropologist adds.

AAPA Authority Certificate support and sacred site advice

An Authority Certificate from the Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority is the only legal protection against prosecution under the Northern Territory Aboriginal Sacred Sites Act 1989. Connected2Country has worked on sacred site surveys with AAPA and custodians from both the land council and the proponent side, and helps proponents get the certificate, understand its conditions and stay compliant on site.

What is included

  • Pre-application advice: whether a certificate is needed, what area to apply over and how to describe the works to avoid re-applications
  • Desktop review of AAPA register abstracts, previous certificates and anthropological literature for the project area
  • Support to custodian consultation and sacred site surveys, including attendance on Country
  • Plain-English interpretation of Authority Certificate conditions, restricted work areas and buffers for engineers and site supervisors
  • Sacred site awareness inductions, site marking protocols and compliance inspections during construction
  • Advice on variations, amendments and incidents, and on registering sites where custodians request it

Who it is for

Developers and contractors applying for or working under an Authority Certificate in Darwin, Palmerston, the rural area and across the NT; environmental consultants preparing EIS culture and heritage chapters; NT Government and Defence project teams.

Pricing

Pre-application advice and desktop reviews are fixed price. Consultation support and construction compliance are on an agreed day rate.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an AAPA Authority Certificate for my development?

If your works could affect a sacred site, a certificate is the only way to be sure you are protected. In practice, most subdivisions, infrastructure projects and works near the coast, waterways, rock outcrops or significant trees in the Darwin region apply for one. AAPA charges fees based on project value; we can advise before you apply whether the application is worth it and how to scope it.

What is a restricted work area?

A restricted work area (RWA) is an area defined in an Authority Certificate within which particular works are prohibited or conditioned to protect a sacred site. Conditions commonly limit ground disturbance, vegetation clearing, access or vehicle movement. The site itself is confidential; the RWA is what the proponent must manage.

Can Connected2Country issue a sacred site clearance?

No. Only AAPA issues Authority Certificates. Connected2Country prepares you for the application, supports the custodian consultation and helps you comply with the conditions.

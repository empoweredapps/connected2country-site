Cultural heritage and cultural values assessments

Cultural heritage is more than archaeological sites. Connected2Country assesses the intangible, living and historical values that Aboriginal people hold for a place, alongside the tangible heritage protected under the Heritage Act 2011 (NT), and presents them in a form that planners, assessors and courts can use.

What is included

  • Desktop cultural heritage assessments: register searches, literature review, previous surveys and constraints mapping
  • Ethnographic and cultural values assessments with Traditional Owners, including intangible values, story, kinship and ecological knowledge
  • Archaeological survey coordination and field recording, with specialist archaeologists engaged for larger programs
  • Culture and heritage chapters for NT EPA environmental impact statements and referrals
  • Heritage Act 2011 advice: declared places, heritage significance, works approvals and Heritage Council submissions
  • Ethno-ecology research linking cultural values to flora, fauna, water and seasonal use

Who it is for

Proponents of subdivisions, infrastructure, Defence, energy and resource projects; environmental consultancies that need the heritage component delivered locally; land councils and Aboriginal corporations documenting values on their own Country.

Pricing

Desktop assessments are fixed price. Field assessments are quoted on area, access and number of Traditional Owner participants.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the Heritage Act and the Sacred Sites Act?

The Heritage Act 2011 (NT) protects declared heritage places and objects, plus all Aboriginal and Macassan archaeological places and objects, and is administered by the Heritage Branch. The Northern Territory Aboriginal Sacred Sites Act 1989 protects sacred sites, which are places of significance under Aboriginal tradition, and is administered by AAPA. A project often needs to deal with both.

Do you do archaeology as well as anthropology?

Yes. Helen Haritos holds an honours degree in archaeology and has conducted archaeological surveys in the NT, and partners with specialist archaeologists for larger programs. Most Top End projects need both disciplines working together.

Can the assessment be used in an EIS?

Yes. Assessments are written to NT EPA environmental factor guidance for culture and heritage and are structured so they drop straight into an EIS or referral.

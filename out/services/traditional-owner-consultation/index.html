Traditional Owner and Native Title holder consultation

Most heritage, sacred site, planning and environmental approvals in the Territory come back to the same question: have the right Traditional Owners been asked, properly, and is there a record of what they said? C2C works out who speaks for the Country in question, sets up a process that suits them rather than the project program, runs the meetings and site visits, and writes it up in a form that AAPA, the land councils, the Heritage Branch and lawyers will accept.

What is included

  • Identification of Traditional Owners, custodians and Native Title holders for the project area, drawing on land council, AAPA, Native Title and published anthropological records
  • Consultation design: who, where, in what order, with what information, and how consent or objection is recorded
  • Facilitation of meetings, on-Country site visits and family-group discussions, with interpreters and cultural advisers where needed
  • Consultation registers, meeting records, consent and conditions documentation suitable for EIS, AAPA, Heritage Branch and Development Consent Authority submissions
  • Ongoing liaison through construction, including cultural monitors and incident response
  • Advice on engagement under the Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976, the Native Title Act 1993 and land council processes

Who it is for

Developers, civil head contractors and project managers with a Darwin or Top End project that needs Traditional Owner input; heritage and environmental consultancies that need a Darwin-based anthropologist for the consultation component; NT Government agencies, Defence contractors and utilities.

Pricing

Scoped and quoted before work starts. Defined consultation programs are fixed price; open-ended engagement is on an agreed day rate with meeting payments and interpreter costs at cost.

Frequently asked questions

Who are the Traditional Owners for Darwin?

The Larrakia people are the recognised Traditional Owners of the Darwin region, including Darwin, Palmerston and much of the rural area. Who speaks for a specific place within Larrakia country, and for places on the margins of neighbouring estates, is a matter for proper anthropological inquiry rather than a single list, which is what the consultation process establishes.

Is Traditional Owner consultation legally required?

It depends on the approval pathway. AAPA Authority Certificates are issued on the basis of consultation with custodians; works on Aboriginal land require land council and Traditional Owner consent under the Land Rights Act; NT EPA assessments and Development Consent Authority processes expect it; and Native Title future act processes require notification or negotiation. Even where it is not a formal requirement, documented consultation is the single best protection against delay later.

How long does consultation take?

Allow six to twelve weeks for a typical Darwin development, longer for remote communities or where sorry business, ceremony or the Wet season affects availability. Early engagement is the only reliable way to shorten the critical path.

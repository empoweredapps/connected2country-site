AI and digital services for cultural heritage

C2C uses AI tools for the desktop side of heritage work: reading registers and old reports, drafting from field notes, keeping registers current. It makes that part faster and cheaper. It does not replace going on Country or talking to people, and it raises real questions about where cultural knowledge ends up. C2C helps Traditional Owner organisations set the rules for that, and helps proponents and consultancies use the tools without getting it wrong.

What is included

  • AI-assisted desktop assessments: rapid review of registers, reports, literature and archives, verified by the anthropologist before anything is relied on
  • Digital recording and archiving of cultural knowledge, oral histories and collections, with access controls set by custodians
  • Indigenous Data Sovereignty and AI governance advice for land councils, Aboriginal corporations and ranger groups, aligned with CARE principles and Maiam nayri Wingara guidance
  • Advice to proponents and consultancies on what AI can and cannot do in heritage compliance, and what regulators and Traditional Owners will accept
  • Digitisation and cataloguing support for community museums, keeping places and archives, building on work with the Patakijiyali Museum on the Tiwi Islands
  • Plain-language cultural protocols for AI use within organisations working with Aboriginal knowledge

Who it is for

Aboriginal corporations, land councils and ranger groups deciding how their knowledge is handled; museums and keeping places; environmental and heritage consultancies and proponents who want faster desktop work without compromising the fieldwork.

Pricing

Advisory work is quoted per brief. AI-assisted desktop assessments are fixed price and typically cost less than conventional desktop studies.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI replace an anthropologist for Traditional Owner consultation?

No. Consultation is a relationship between people about Country, and the legal and cultural weight of the outcome rests on that. AI is useful for the desktop work around it: finding and summarising what has already been recorded, drafting documents from field notes, and keeping registers current.

What is Indigenous Data Sovereignty?

The right of Indigenous peoples to govern the collection, ownership and application of data about their people, lands, knowledge and resources. For cultural heritage it means custodians decide what is recorded, who holds it, who can see it and whether it may be used to train or query AI systems.

Is cultural information safe in AI tools?

Only if the tool, the contract and the workflow are chosen for it. Public AI services may retain and reuse inputs. Connected2Country advises on tools and settings that do not train on your data, on what should never be entered at all, and on written protocols so staff know the difference.
